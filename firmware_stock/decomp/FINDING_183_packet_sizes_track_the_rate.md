# #183: the packet sizes track the rate — and the 44.1 kHz clock word is 6.5 ppm low

2026-08-05, build 0x0034, both units on 192.168.1.76. No flash.

## The question

The capture endpoint's packet sizes are generated by the DMA, not by the 8051,
and they had only ever been looked at at 48 kHz, where they are 288 bytes — 48
samples × 6. Nothing in this repo established that they follow the declared rate
at all. If they were fixed at 288, a stream declared as 44.1 kHz would deliver
48 samples per frame and run **~8.8% fast** — a defect that no loopback measures,
because a loopback at a single rate cannot see it.

## They track it

usbmon on the capture endpoint, unit A, 60 s of `arecord` at 44.1 kHz:

    54040 packets x 264 bytes   (44 samples)
     5978 packets x 270 bytes   (45 samples)
    ------------------------------------------
    2,646,770 samples / 60,018 frames = 44.099603 samples/frame

The declared rate is 44.1. The delivered rate is 44.0996, i.e. **-9.0 ppm**. The
hypothesis is refuted: the DMA generates a genuine fractional rate by mixing 44-
and 45-sample packets in roughly a 9:1 ratio, exactly as a compliant 44.1 kHz
full-speed stream must.

The 48 kHz control is more interesting than expected. It is *not* a uniform 288:

        9 packets x 282 bytes   (47 samples)
    14997 packets x 288 bytes   (48 samples)
        7 packets x 294 bytes   (49 samples)
    ------------------------------------------
    720,622 samples / 15,013 frames = 47.999867 -> -2.78 ppm

Even at an "exact" rate the DMA is tracking a real clock rather than emitting a
constant, and it drops or adds a sample when the accumulated phase demands one.
That is the same mechanism as at 44.1 kHz, running with a much smaller
fractional part. **Declaring 288 the packet size at 48 kHz was a description of
the common case, not of the mechanism** — which is why the 44.1 kHz check was
worth running rather than reasoning about.

## Cross-check: two paths, one answer

The packet histogram and telemetry block 11 measure the same physical quantity
through nothing in common — one counts bytes at the host, the other counts MCLKO
edges against SOF on-chip:

| unit A | packets | block 11 |
|---|---|---|
| 48 kHz | -2.78 ppm | -2.54 ppm |
| 44.1 kHz | -9.0 ppm | -9.03 ppm |

## The second result: it is the frequency word, not the crystal

Unit A reads -2.54 ppm at 48 kHz but -9.03 ppm at 44.1 kHz. That is the same
crystal in both readings, so a ~6.5 ppm rate-dependent term has to come from
somewhere else. Running the same pair on unit B separates the two:

| unit | 48 kHz | 44.1 kHz | 44.1 - 48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (`RK10874600Q`) | -2.54 ppm | -9.03 ppm | **-6.49 ppm** |
| B (`RK1672500M`)  | -6.76 ppm | -13.36 ppm | **-6.60 ppm** |

The two units' crystals differ by 4 ppm, and their 44.1-vs-48 offsets agree to
0.11 ppm. A term that is common-mode across two units is not a crystal; it is
the **24-bit ACG frequency word for 44.1 kHz** — `ACG1FRQ = 0x204B6A`, which
this firmware takes from stock (Rev 20 fcn.0x0728 @ 0x075F/0x0765/0x076B, Rev 22
@ 0x079A/0x07A0/0x07A6). The 48 kHz word `0x0FA861` is a different ACG mode and
carries a different, smaller quantisation error.

So **the stock 44.1 kHz clock is intrinsically ~6.5 ppm slow on any unit**, and
always has been. This was not measurable before block 11 existed.

The unit-to-unit differential survives as a control: A-B is +4.22 ppm at 48 kHz
and +4.33 ppm at 44.1 kHz, against the +4.263 +/- 0.989 ppm that #181/#182
measured host-side by a completely different method.

## What is not being done about it

Nothing, deliberately. 6.5 ppm is far inside any audio tolerance — a crystal
spec of +/-50 ppm is ordinary, and both units' own crystals contribute more than
this does. Changing a stock-cited word to chase it would trade a measured
citation for a computed one and buy inaudibility.

It is also now self-correcting on playback: #186's feedback endpoint reports the
**measured** rate, not the nominal one, so the host resamples to whatever the
ACG actually produces. The 10.14 format's own quantisation is 1.27 ppm, five
times finer than this offset, so the error is inside what the feedback loop
already communicates.

## Method note

The first pass at the 44.1 kHz number was wrong — an arithmetic slip put it at
-5.4 ppm, which disagreed with block 11's -9.0 and looked like a real divergence
between the two measurement paths. It was worth resolving rather than reporting:
the agreement between host-side and device-side is the only reason the 6.5 ppm
common-mode term above can be attributed to the frequency word instead of to
whichever instrument was believed.
